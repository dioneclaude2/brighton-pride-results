Overall verdict

Did the campaign work?

Yes, and paid support did most of the work: 11 boosted posts drove 479,459 of the 677,288 total views (71%), against 197,829 views (29%) from 4 fully organic posts. Spend split heavily toward the on-the-ground event ($10,534.82, 76%) over Meta ads ($3,235.62, 24%) — and the ads were the more efficient line item by far: $6.75 CPM on ads versus $53.25 when the event cost is measured against organic views alone. Blended across everything, the campaign cost $20.33 per 1,000 views. Two posts (an organic Reel and an organic Carousel) don't have view counts recorded yet in the source sheet — flagged below rather than assumed as zero.

Bottom line

Reading this together

The campaign generated 677,288 views from 15 posts on $13,770.44 in total spend — 71% of views came from the 11 boosted posts. The event itself was the larger cost line ($10,534.82, 76.5%) versus Meta ads ($3,235.62, 24.5%), but the ads were dramatically more efficient per view ($6.75 CPM vs. $53.25 when the event cost is measured against organic views alone). For next time: two posts are still missing view counts in the source sheet and should be filled in before this report is treated as final, and it's worth testing whether shifting more budget toward ads (rather than event production) improves the blended CPM further.

Methodology — how this report was built
  1. All figures pulled directly from the campaign Google Sheet: the "Brighton" tab (per-post views, spend, boosted status) and the "Total Spend" tab (event cost, blended totals, CPM calculations).
  2. Organic CPM = Event spend ÷ organic views × 1,000. Ads CPM = ad spend ÷ boosted views × 1,000. Blended CPM = total spend ÷ total views × 1,000 — all as computed in the source sheet, cross-checked against the per-post totals.
  3. Two organic posts (Carousel #3 and Carousel #15) have no recorded view count in the source sheet. Shown as "—" rather than assumed zero.
  4. No captions, dates, or engagement metrics (likes/comments/shares) were provided in this version of the source data, so posts are labeled by format and sequence only, and no engagement-rate figures are shown.
  5. All figures are in USD as reported in the source sheet.

Team feedback

What's working, what's not, and what's next

Crystal, Head of Marketing — drawing on lessons from the ASAP Hong Kong event.

What's working 💛

  • The pride footage is beautifully shot — genuinely strong material to work with.

The gaps ⚠️

  • It's almost all pride footage, barely any product in frame. We need product living in the environment — e.g. holding the toy, it vibrates, parade happening behind. There are loads of ways to grab B-roll product demo shots.
  • Only 15 posts across a 2-day event, well under where we should be.
  • We didn't ride the moment. The trend dies down quick, so we need to post as much as possible during that weekend, not after. We may need to increase our manpower support on remote editors.

What I'd change next time 🔁

  • 🎬 Story-led reels — keep the beautiful pride shots, cut in the B-roll product demo, layer a narrative VO. That's where the strong storytelling comes from.
  • ✂️ Divide & conquer on edit — editor support is the bottleneck. Dione and I have been chatting about bringing on freelance PH editors (lower labour cost), all working in CapCut — best template tool for this.
  • 🎁 Gift creators in advance and provide a strong mini site brief — biggest HK learning. Find the micro + nano creators already going to the event, get product to them early, and send expected deliverables + a mini site brief so they know our direction and expectations.
  • 📋 Plan to realistic shoot time — pick formats we can actually pull off on the day. Street interviews with games, that kind of thing.
