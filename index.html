Verdict

What each team's numbers actually mean

Mid — biggest numbers, weakest resonance

Lauren's Team

Got 92% of the entire ad budget ($33,734 of $36,687) and posted 11 times, so it leads on every raw total. But its engagement rate on reach is only 0.28% — the lowest of the three teams — meaning the huge reach is mostly bought, not earned. 38% of its ad spend ($13,758.81) sits on a single post whose ad attribution is unconfirmed (duplicate ad name in the source data), so even the paid-performance number carries real uncertainty.

Did well — most efficient, most resonant

Content Forge

Ran on less than 1% of the total ad budget ($198.56) yet produced the best engagement rate of the two well-resourced teams (0.87% of reach — ~3× Lauren's Team) and added extra reach from Facebook cross-posting no other team captured. Pride-centered content clearly resonated per viewer — it just was never given real paid support, so its ceiling with a proper budget is untested.

Inconclusive — too little activity

Team Nancy

Only 2 posts, zero ad spend, reach under 9,000. Its engagement rate (1.07% of reach) is technically the highest of all three teams, but the sample is too small to call that a real signal. The real story here is volume: this team barely showed up in the campaign.

Read with caution

Key problems & risks in this data

  1. Ad budget is extremely lopsided, so raw totals aren't a fair team comparison. Lauren's Team got 92% of all ad spend, Content Forge under 1%, Team Nancy 0%. Comparing total views/reach across teams mostly measures who got budget, not who made better content.
  2. A large chunk of Lauren's Team's paid results is unverified. $13,758.81 (38% of that team's ad spend) is attributed to one post based on a duplicated ad name in the source CSV — the true split between the two ad line items for that post is not confirmed.
  3. $2,754.49 in ad spend (7.5% of the total tracked budget) doesn't match any of the 21 organic posts provided. That money is essentially untraceable in this report — either it ran on content outside the links given, or there's a naming/caption mismatch worth chasing down with whoever ran the ads.
  4. Team Nancy's sample size (2 posts) is too small to draw conclusions from. Its strong engagement rate could be genuine or could be noise from a single well-performing post — there isn't enough volume either way.
  5. Views aren't fully apples-to-apples across post types. Instagram's own disclaimer confirms Reels' organic views exclude ad-delivered plays, but image/carousel posts' views may already include some ad-driven plays — so Content Forge's organic-view totals (which include several image posts) may be very slightly inflated relative to Lauren's Team's, which is almost entirely Reels.
Bottom line

The playbook for next time

The campaign's headline numbers are carried almost entirely by Lauren's Team, but that's a budget story more than a content story — give any team 92% of the ad spend and it will lead on views and reach. On a level playing field (engagement per person reached), Content Forge's Pride-centered content actually performed best, and it did so on essentially no ad budget. If this campaign runs again, the clearest opportunity is to put real ad spend behind Content Forge's content and see if it holds up at scale, resolve the duplicate ad-name and unmatched-spend issues with whoever manages the ad account so future reports are cleaner, and give Team Nancy a real posting volume before judging its output.

Methodology — how this report was built (10 notes)
  1. Organic metrics (Views, Reach, Likes, Comments, Saves, Shares) were pulled directly from each post's native Instagram Insights panel (admin view on hellonancy_official), not estimated.
  2. For Reels, Instagram's own disclaimer states native Views EXCLUDE plays delivered as an ad, so the Views figure in Lauren's Team and most Content Forge rows is organic-only, separate from paid Ad Views.
  3. For image/carousel posts, Instagram's disclaimer differs and does NOT state ad plays are excluded — so for those posts (e.g. Spreading the love at Brighton Pride), the Views figure may already include some ad-driven views. True organic-only views for that post are likely closer to (Views − Ad Views).
  4. Boosted vs Organic status was determined ONLY by matching each post's caption text against the 12 ad names in the CSV — not assumed. Instagram's own "Ad" section on each post was checked but found to appear on every post regardless of ad history, so it was not usable as a signal.
  5. "Brighton Pride with the Nancy team" appears TWICE in the ad CSV with different spend/views. Only one post (Lauren's Team, DbgHLVGjTMr) has a caption starting with this exact phrase, so both ad line items are tentatively attributed to it — flagged as unconfirmed rather than split arbitrarily.
  6. Two ads ("Weather forecast for Brighton Pride", $2,748.09 spend; "More color, more laughter, more freedom, more pleasure", $6.40 spend) have no matching caption in any of the 21 links provided across all 3 teams. Not matched to any post — flagged as unresolved rather than guessed.
  7. Content Forge link #1 (DbdAn7Sshf3) was submitted twice in the original list — counted once.
  8. Several Content Forge posts are cross-posted to Facebook; their FB Views/Reactions/Comments are recorded separately and are NOT included in the IG Views/Likes/Comments totals.
  9. Dates for posts without an exact date shown (Instagram displayed "Xd"/"Xw" instead of a calendar date) were computed from that relative timestamp against Aug 18, 2026 — treat as approximate.
  10. Currency: all ad spend figures are in HKD as reported in the source CSV.
